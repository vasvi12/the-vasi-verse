## Vision

This page is probably the most important page for getting PM interviews. Builds prove execution. Case Studies prove thinking. A recruiter can teach execution. They cannot easily teach product thinking. This page should feel like opening McKinsey strategy reports × Apple's editorial design × Linear's documentation. Not a blog. Not Medium. Not Notion. A premium collection of thinking.

## Overall Experience

The moment someone lands here they should think "This person genuinely enjoys thinking about products." Not "She made four assignments."

## Layout

Navigation → Hero → Featured Case Study → Case Study Grid → Why I Write Case Studies → Footer. Unlike Builds, this page introduces one featured study followed by the collection.

## Success Criteria

A recruiter should leave this page convinced that you can structure ambiguous problems, understand frameworks without overusing jargon, communicate clearly, and think before you build.

## Improvement Note (Case Titles)

Rather than labeling these simply as Spotify / Uber / Blinkit, give each case study a case title, just like a consulting firm or internal product team would:

- Spotify — Rethinking Music Discovery
- Blinkit — Beyond 10-Minute Delivery
- Uber — Balancing a Two-Sided Marketplace
- HootMe — Designing Network Effects for Real-World Communities

Then the company name becomes secondary metadata. This shifts the focus from "I analyzed Spotify" to "I investigated a product problem," which is exactly what PM recruiters care about.
